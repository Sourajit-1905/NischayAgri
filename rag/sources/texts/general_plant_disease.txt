# General Principles of Plant Disease Management

## The Disease Triangle
Plant disease occurs when three conditions are simultaneously met: a susceptible host plant, a virulent pathogen, and a favourable environment. Management strategies aim to disrupt one or more sides of this triangle.

## Integrated Disease Management
Effective plant disease management relies on integrating multiple tactics rather than relying on any single approach. The main pillars are:

### Resistant Varieties
Planting resistant or tolerant varieties is the most economical and environmentally sustainable management strategy. Consult local extension recommendations for varieties suited to your region and disease pressure.

### Cultural Practices
- Crop rotation: Rotating to non-host crops breaks the pathogen cycle and reduces inoculum levels.
- Sanitation: Removing and destroying infected plant debris reduces overwintering inoculum.
- Planting density and canopy management: Adequate spacing and pruning improve air circulation, reducing leaf wetness duration.
- Irrigation management: Drip or furrow irrigation reduces leaf wetness compared to overhead irrigation.
- Timing: Early or late planting dates can help crops escape peak disease periods.

### Biological Control
Several biocontrol agents (e.g., Trichoderma species, Bacillus subtilis) are available for soil-borne and foliar diseases. These are most effective as part of an integrated programme rather than sole management tools.

### Chemical Control
Fungicides and bactericides are important tools when other tactics are insufficient. Key principles:
- Apply preventively before disease onset under high-risk conditions.
- Rotate fungicide classes (modes of action) to delay resistance development.
- Follow label instructions for rates, intervals, and pre-harvest intervals.
- Resistance to some fungicide classes (e.g., metalaxyl, some strobilurins) is widespread; monitor local resistance reports.

## When to Seek Expert Help
Consult a qualified plant pathologist or agricultural extension officer when:
- Disease identity is uncertain.
- Losses are severe or occurring rapidly.
- Standard management practices are not effective.
- Novel symptoms appear that do not match known diseases.

## Diagnostic Resources
- Local agricultural extension services
- University plant diagnostic laboratories
- National plant protection organisations (e.g., USDA APHIS, EPPO)

## Safety Notice
This document provides general guidance only. All pesticide applications must follow local label instructions and comply with regulations. This guidance does not replace advice from a qualified agricultural professional. Always identify the disease correctly before initiating a management programme.
